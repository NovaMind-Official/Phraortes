How to write an electron configuration

An electron configuration is a list of where an atom keeps its electrons. Follow the filling order and it is a few lines of counting.

CHEMISTRY · 5 MIN READ

Try it: tap any element

The filling order

Electrons fill the lowest-energy subshells first. The order is

1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p 7s

Each subshell has a capacity: s holds 2 electrons, p holds 6, d holds 10 and f holds 14. The number of electrons to place equals the atomic number for a neutral atom.

Examples

AtomElectronsConfiguration
Oxygen81s² 2s² 2p⁴
Chlorine171s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵
Iron261s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶

The shortcut

Write the previous noble gas in brackets and continue from there. Iron becomes [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶ and chlorine becomes [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵. The bracket stands for all the filled inner shells.

Exceptions you will meet

A half-filled or completely filled d subshell is unusually stable, so a few atoms move an electron to reach it:

  • Chromium (24): [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵, not 4s² 3d⁴
  • Copper (29): [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰, not 4s² 3d⁹
  • Gold (79): [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s¹

Check gold: xenon accounts for 54 electrons, then 14 + 10 + 1 = 25 more, which makes 79.

Ions

When an atom loses electrons, they leave from the outermost shell first, which is the 4s before the 3d in iron. So Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶, not [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴.
