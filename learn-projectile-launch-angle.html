Is 45° really the best launch angle?

On flat ground, yes. But the moment you launch from a height, the best angle drops below 45°.

PHYSICS · 4 MIN READ

Try it: change speed, angle and launch height

Level ground

If you launch and land at the same height, with no air resistance, the range is

R = v₀² · sin(2θ) / g

sin(2θ) is largest, equal to 1, when 2θ = 90°, so θ = 45°. Two angles that add up to 90° give the same range. For example, 35° and 55° land in the same place, one with a flat fast arc and one with a high lob.

Worked example

Launch speed v₀ = 18 m/s and g = 9.81 m/s².

AngleRange
35°31.0 m
45°33.0 m
55°31.0 m

Time in the air and the peak height follow from the vertical motion:

T = 2 v₀ sin θ / g
H = v₀² sin²θ / (2g)

Launching from a height

Throw from a cliff, a roof or even shoulder height and the ball has extra distance to fall, which buys it extra time in the air. The best angle then becomes

θ = arctan( v₀ / √(v₀² + 2 g h) )

With v₀ = 18 m/s and a launch height of 2 m, that is about 43°. The higher you launch from, the smaller the best angle gets.

Real projectiles also fight air resistance, which lowers the best angle too. The formulas here assume none.

What to try

  • Set the height to 0 and sweep the angle. Watch the range peak at 45°.
  • Raise the launch height and see the peak shift to a smaller angle.
  • Find two angles that land in the same spot.
