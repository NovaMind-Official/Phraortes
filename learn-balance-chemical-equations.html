How to balance a chemical equation

Atoms are never created or destroyed in a reaction. Balancing an equation is just making the atom counts match on both sides.

CHEMISTRY · 4 MIN READ

Try it: type any unbalanced equation

The idea

A reaction rearranges atoms. So every element must appear the same number of times on the left (the reactants) and on the right (the products). You get there by changing the coefficients, the big numbers in front of each formula, and never the small subscripts inside a formula.

Changing a subscript changes the substance. H₂O is water; H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide. Only change the numbers in front.

A four-step method

  1. Write the correct formula for every substance.
  2. Count the atoms of each element on both sides.
  3. Balance one element at a time. Start with elements that appear in only one compound on each side, and leave hydrogen, oxygen and lone elements for last.
  4. Recount everything. If a fraction helped you, multiply the whole equation to clear it.

Example 1: aluminium and oxygen

Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃

Left: 1 Al, 2 O. Right: 2 Al, 3 O. Oxygen is the awkward one: 2 on the left, 3 on the right. The smallest number both divide into is 6, so make six oxygen atoms on each side: 3 O₂ and 2 Al₂O₃.

Al + 3 O₂ → 2 Al₂O₃

Now the right side has 4 Al, so put a 4 in front of the Al on the left.

4 Al + 3 O₂ → 2 Al₂O₃

Check: 4 Al and 6 O on each side. Balanced.

Example 2: burning propane

C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Do carbon first: 3 C on the left means 3 CO₂. Then hydrogen: 8 H means 4 H₂O. Now count oxygen on the right: 3 × 2 + 4 = 10, so you need 5 O₂ on the left.

C₃H₈ + 5 O₂ → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O

Common slips

  • Balancing oxygen first when it appears in several compounds. Leave it for last.
  • Forgetting to multiply a coefficient through the whole formula: 2 Al₂O₃ means 4 Al and 6 O.
  • Not rechecking at the end. One quick recount catches most mistakes.
